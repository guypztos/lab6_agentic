# Scene 19 — Aloha is everywhere

## Narration

> And Aloha didn't stay in Hawaii. Bob Metcalfe read about it, and it inspired him to create Ethernet. Its ideas live on in Wi-Fi. And remember the phone at the bus stop? When it first reaches out to a cell tower, it uses a form of slotted Aloha. So do R F I D tags, satellite links, and tiny smart sensors.

## What the viewer must walk away with
Aloha isn't history trivia. **It's running in the viewer's pocket right now.**

## Real-life layer
- **Metcalfe:** a 1970s office desk with a printed paper labelled "ALOHA" and a coax cable being plugged in. Caption
  *Bob Metcalfe · Ethernet, 1973*.
- **Wi-Fi:** a home router with Wi-Fi arcs, phones and laptops around it.
- **The payoff:** back to **the bus stop**. The Commuter's phone wakes up and sends a tiny first "hello" burst to the
  rooftop **cell tower**. A slot grid briefly overlays the burst. Label *random access: slotted Aloha*.
- **Montage:** an **RFID** tag at a store checkout gate (many tags answering at once), a **satellite** dish under the
  sky, and a **smart sensor** (a small box on a farm fence or a parking spot, e.g. LoRaWAN).

## Theory layer
Each real-life tile carries a small icon of the **slot grid** or a **random arrival dot stream** in its corner, so the
viewer reads "same idea" across all of them.

## Bridge: real life → theory
The Commuter's phone burst **lands on a slot** in a mini slot grid next to the tower. It's the same violet slots from
Scenes 16–17, now in the real world.
