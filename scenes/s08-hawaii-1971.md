# Scene 08 — Hawaii, 1971

**Part 3 · Aloha: the idea** · **Audio:** 22.9 s · **Starts at:** 2:01 · **Composition:** `S08Hawaii1971` · **Audio file:** `build/narration/s08.wav`

## Narration

> In nineteen seventy-one, a team at the University of Hawaii, led by Norman Abramson, faced exactly this problem. They wanted to connect computer terminals spread across the islands to one central computer in Honolulu. Phone lines were expensive and unreliable, so they used radio. Every terminal talked to the hub on the same frequency. They called it Aloha-net.

## What the viewer must walk away with
The real origin story, and the **many terminals → one hub over one shared radio frequency** setup.

## Real-life layer
A stylised **map of the Hawaiian islands** on a deep-blue ocean in a warm, slightly retro 1970s print style (muted
teal and sand colours, still inside our palette rules).
- **Honolulu (Oʻahu):** a hub building with a tall antenna mast and a big 1970s mainframe drawn as a boxy cabinet with tape reels.
- **Terminals** on other islands and campuses: boxy teletype or terminal icons with small antennas.
- **Phone lines:** a telephone-pole line between islands gets a price tag "$$$" and crackles, then fades. Radio arcs replace it.
- All radio arcs from terminals to the hub are the **same colour**, which shows the same frequency.

## Theory layer
The map simplifies into the reusable **hub-and-terminals** diagram: one hub node in the centre, 6–8 terminal nodes
around it, arcs inward. This diagram is reused in Scenes 09, 11 and 12.

## Bridge: real life → theory
The islands **fade away** while the terminal and hub markers stay. Their positions ease into a tidy ring. The map
becomes the diagram without a cut.

## Notes / pitfalls
- Historically the hub's replies went out on a **second** frequency. The terminals shared the *inbound* one.
  Don't draw the replies on the shared colour. Scene 09 uses a different colour for ACKs.
- Narration says "Aloha-net" for pronunciation. Spell it **ALOHAnet** on screen.
