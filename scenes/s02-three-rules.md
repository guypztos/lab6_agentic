# Scene 02 — The three rules

**Part 1 · The Poisson process** · **Audio:** 16.8 s · **Starts at:** 0:11 · **Composition:** `S02ThreeRules` · **Audio file:** `build/narration/s02.wav`

## Narration

> It's called a Poisson process, and it has three rules. First, events arrive at a steady average rate, called lambda. Say, ten buses an hour. Second, they're independent. Waited five minutes already? The bus is no more likely to come now. Third, they arrive one at a time.

## What the viewer must walk away with
The definition: a **steady average rate λ**, **independence** (no memory), and **one at a time**.

## Real-life layer
Back at the **bus stop**, now as a medium shot of the Commuter. Busy city traffic passes, so buses don't keep to a
timetable. A small digital **"buses this hour" counter** sits on the shelter.
- **Rule 1:** a time-lapse of an hour. Buses come and go at irregular moments, and the counter ticks to about 10.
- **Rule 2:** the Commuter **checks their watch**. A "waited: 5:00" timer appears above their head, then an
  arrow pointing to "next minute" with a probability meter that **doesn't move** as the timer grows.
- **Rule 3:** two buses approach close together but still arrive one after the other, never at the exact same instant.

## Theory layer
A single **timeline** (carried over from Scene 01) runs along the bottom third, and the buses drop onto it as dots.
- Rule 1: label the timeline **λ = 10 per hour**, shown as the symbol *λ* with the word "lambda" under it on first appearance.
- Rule 2: highlight the gap after the last dot. Replay the same moment three times with three different "next dot"
  positions to show that the future doesn't depend on the past. Caption: **no memory**.
- Rule 3: zoom deep into the timeline. Even at high zoom, two close dots stay two separate dots.

## Bridge: real life → theory
Each bus arrival **shrinks into a blue dot** that falls onto the timeline below, the same move as Scene 01. By Rule 3
the street is gone and only the timeline is left, which hands off to the abstract version.

## Notes / pitfalls
- We say buses are random *because of city traffic*. Show traffic to make that believable. Scene 03 then contrasts
  this with a strictly scheduled train.
- "No memory" is the most counter-intuitive idea in Part 1. Give the flat meter a full beat on screen.
